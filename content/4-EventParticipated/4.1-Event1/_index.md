### What I learned

- The speakers kept coming back to one order of work: agree on the problem and the data flow, then write code. A bot that only "runs" is not finished; someone has to pay for it and keep it safe.
- Walking through real test questions showed me that splitting a requirement into small pieces is most of the work.
- The career talk told me that recruiters care about what I actually built and can explain, not the list of services I have heard of.

### Applying it to my work / the Cost Guard project

Our project is not a bot, but the same order helped. Before writing any Lambda, our team agreed on the three layers: a daily stop at 22:00, a cost meter every 5 minutes, and a Budgets backstop. Then I built the foundation and Layer 1 first, since everything else depends on it. The point about cost also fit well: Cost Guard exists to protect the Free Plan credit, so I checked the pricing of every piece we added.
